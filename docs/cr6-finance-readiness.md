# CR6 — pricing and finance readiness audit

Status: **technical HOLD for unrestricted paid use**. This is a staging architecture and test assessment, not approval to take customer money. Production and historical stabilization were not changed.

## Evidence and classification

The staging project `hvcggnuptrcsxtrcjnre` has 84 migrations (latest `202608120014`), one active `tour_pricing_config` row, and all 12 core commercial settings in that row are `NULL`. It has no pricing plans, supplier-cost rows, allocations, proposals, acceptances, journey accounts, settlements, accounting transactions, accounting attachments, or cancellation cases. CR5 found zero verified commercial accommodations, transport providers, or guides; the 10/10/6 supplier catalogue records are demo/seed. No staging finance row can be classified as real/verified, real/unverified, or synthetic transactional evidence. The one blank configuration row is unconfigured system data, not a commercial rate. Unknown real-rate evidence: none found. No records were deleted or normalized.

The public Journey Builder range is a planning benchmark, not a supplier-backed quotation or contracted rate. Its display cannot establish commercial readiness.

## Source of truth and lifecycle

1. Staff-associated `journey_supplier_allocations` record service, supplier, quantity, rate snapshot, currency, supplier cost and optional manual selling price. A pricing plan or custom rate can feed an allocation; CR5 requires verified rates before real use. Allocation `supplier_cost` is an expected cost, not cash paid.
2. `allocationCommercialSnapshot` reads the authoritative rows and active `tour_pricing_config` on the server. `calculateAllocationCommercials` sums active allocation costs, operations, administration and contingency into internal cost; applies fixed/percentage service fees; then prices against a **gross-margin** target. Explicit service selling prices act as a manual selling floor. The result includes actual gross profit and actual margin. A margin is profit divided by selling price; markup is profit divided by cost. The code's target is margin, not markup. No active proposal-API discount operation exists. The dormant package-engine adjustment type is not passed by the live package service, so it is not an approved discount workflow.
3. Proposal generation rejects incomplete allocation scope, missing service rate/quantity, missing cost or selling price, mixed allocation currencies, and now missing relevant commercial settings. It validates deposit and due-date arithmetic before saving versioned commercial and customer snapshots. The accepted proposal version is immutable; later allocation changes do not resync its account basis.
4. A draft/sent/accepted proposal does not by itself record money received. The existing accounting integrity commands create/activate the account and supplier settlements on the first positive traveller deposit. The traveller pays The Ceylon Edition, which pays suppliers; no traveller-to-supplier payment path was found. `journey_accounts` and posted `accounting_transactions` are financial truth; receipts/refunds/settlements derive collected, outstanding, payable and paid positions. Enquiry/journey operational status remains separate.
5. Payment, supplier settlement, waiver and refund commands are service-side, RBAC checked, idempotency guarded and audited with immutable posted transaction/reversal semantics. Supplier cost, margins, bank/payment references and private finance notes are excluded from the customer-safe proposal DTO. Finance permissions differ from journey-designer permissions. Public and unauthenticated admin boundaries remain protected.

## Precision, currency and schedule limits

The application uses JavaScript `number`, rounds displayed/calculated totals to two decimals, and uses PostgreSQL unconstrained `numeric` for important monetary columns/RPC amounts. Tests show the current calculator rounds `0.1 + 0.2` to `0.30`, but this does **not** prove end-to-end cent precision: direct database commands and some API input schemas can accept a third decimal, while database comparisons use small tolerances. This is a material finance-schema gap. Do not silently change historical rows or money types during CR6.

The proposal enforces one allocation currency; account settlement enforces its account currency. No FX conversion, rate source, conversion-time snapshot or reproducible historical FX calculation exists. A controlled launch therefore needs a founder-approved single traveller/supplier settlement currency and a policy for any supplier invoicing in another currency; do not infer an exchange rate. The current staging configuration says USD but contains no approved values or real rates.

The proposal supports a deposit amount plus deposit and balance due dates, with the balance derived as total minus deposit. CR6 rejects an over-total, fractional-cent or negative deposit and reversed dates. It does **not** have an enforceable arbitrary milestone schedule, due-date collection engine, automatic reminders or payment-provider integration. Cash receipt is manually recorded through guarded accounting commands. Historical sent customer snapshots can show paid amount as of document generation, not a live bank balance; the account ledger is authoritative for current payment status. No deposit percentage, supplier payment timing, cancellation charge, tax, payment-provider fee or refund policy has been approved in this audit.

## Synthetic fixture coverage

Local tests only; no synthetic staging transactions were written. Tests cover multiple supplier costs, target margin versus markup, manual selling floor/actual margin, `0.1 + 0.2` rounding, missing pricing settings, deposit/balance reconciliation, partial/full positions, invalid totals and dates, absence of an active discount API, accepted proposal snapshot source, immutable accounting, duplicate-payment idempotency, refund/overpayment guards, currency mismatch, RBAC and public DTO confidentiality. Existing accounting-integrity, cancellation and proposal-acceptance tests were also run. These source/fixture checks are not a substitute for a later isolated end-to-end transactional test with authorized staging data.

## FINANCE SCHEMA CHANGE PROPOSAL — not implemented

Problem: unrestricted `numeric` amounts and JS floating-point inputs allow sub-cent values, so a two-decimal display can disagree with persisted money or toleranced comparisons. Before paid journeys, review a scoped migration that enforces currency-specific minor-unit precision (for a USD-only initial launch, two decimal places) at **database columns and RPC boundaries**, plus matching API validation. Consider `numeric(p,2)` with explicit checks or integer minor units; do not choose between them without a historical-data audit and rollback design. Inventory all monetary fields and functions, test every posting/reversal/refund/payable path, inspect existing fractional values before conversion, and preserve old immutable proposal/accounting snapshots. Add database rejection tests for sub-cent input, duplicate references and boundary amounts. A separate milestone table is only warranted if the founder approves payment schedules beyond the current deposit/balance model; it would need versioning and exact reconciliation to proposal total. No migration was created here.

## Controlled-launch recommendation and dependencies

Use a constrained, manually supervised first-paid-journey model only after the precision issue is resolved, verified real supplier rates are loaded, and the founder approves the finance-policy matrix. Reasonable *options for approval*, not decisions made here, are a single currency, documented rate validity, founder-approved selling price and margin, a simple deposit/balance plan, manually verified bank receipts, and controlled supplier settlements. Never describe seed costs as contracted rates or use blank configuration defaults as zero-fee policy.

CR5 must supply verified supplier identities, rate sheets, units/quantity bases, currency, validity, inclusions/exclusions, payment/deposit terms, cancellation terms and named verification evidence before a real quotation. CR8/accountant/legal must confirm tax, invoicing and receipts, foreign currency and banking treatment, refund/cancellation terms, supplier withholding, retention and payment-data handling. No Sri Lankan legal or tax position is asserted here.

## Release decision

The fail-closed configuration and proposal-payment corrections improve safety, but staging has no usable real-rate evidence and database-enforced minor-unit precision remains unproven. CR6 cannot claim technical pricing readiness or commercial launch approval. Do not begin CR7/CR8 automatically; obtain human review of this HOLD and the separate policy-decision artifact.
